## How Kapstart calculates

Many German banks will finance the full purchase price of a home, but expect you to pay the closing costs (Kaufnebenkosten) from your own money. Kapstart turns the usual question around: not “what does the house cost on top?”, but “up to which price do my savings cover the closing costs?”

The closing costs are almost entirely percentages of the price: real estate transfer tax (Grunderwerbsteuer, 3.5 to 6.5% depending on the state), notary and land registry (about 2%) and, if a broker is involved, the buyer's share of the commission. So the maximum price is simply your capital divided by the sum of those percentages. The differences between states are large: the same savings buy a noticeably more expensive house in Bavaria than in North Rhine-Westphalia.

The same rules apply to everyone who buys property in Germany, whatever your citizenship or residence permit. The state pages show the tax rate and its history, the customary broker fee and a ready-made “capital → price” table.
